Document Title: IT Support Ticket and Escalation Procedure  
Document ID: IT-SupportProcedure-003  
Category: Helpdesk Operations

**PURPOSE**

Guide employees when an IT problem cannot be resolved through basic troubleshooting and needs further investigation by IT support.

**WHEN TO ESCALATE**

Recommend escalation when:
- Basic troubleshooting has been completed, but the problem persists.
- The issue requires administrator access or configuration changes.
- Multiple employees or an important business service are affected.
- The problem is not covered by the available knowledge base.
- The issue involves a possible security incident.

**INFORMATION TO COLLECT**

Ask for relevant information one question at a time. Collect only what is necessary:
- **Issue:** A brief description of the problem.
- **Affected service or device:** The application, computer, account, or network involved.
- **Impact:** Whether one or multiple employees are affected, if known.
- **Start time:** When the problem began, if known.
- **Error message:** The exact error, if one appeared.
- **Troubleshooting completed:** Steps already attempted and their results.
- **Current status:** Whether the issue is resolved or still occurring.

Never invent missing details. Mark them as unknown or not provided.

**PREPARING AN ESCALATION SUMMARY**

Prepare a concise summary for IT support:

Issue: [Description of the problem]  
Affected service/device: [Known details]  
Impact: [Known impact]  
Error message: [Error or none reported]  
Troubleshooting completed: [Steps and results]  
Current status: [Resolved or unresolved]

**TICKET CREATION RULES**

If the chatbot has no working ticket creation function, explain that the issue needs to be reported to IT support and offer the employee a summary they can copy.

If a ticket creation function is available, submit the ticket only after collecting the required information. Confirm ticket creation and provide a ticket number only after the system reports success.

Never claim that a ticket was created, submitted, assigned, or reviewed when that action has not been confirmed.

**CONVERSATION GUIDELINES**

- Be polite and explain why escalation is needed.
- Ask one clear question at a time.
- Do not repeat questions the employee has already answered.
- Do not guarantee a resolution time unless an official policy provides one.
- Do not ask for passwords or verification codes.
- If the issue is resolved, acknowledge the resolution and stop unnecessary troubleshooting.
- If a security incident is suspected, follow the security incident procedure instead of treating it as an ordinary IT issue.
