Document Title: IT Security Incident Reporting Procedure  
Document ID: IT-SecurityProcedure-001  
Category: Information Security

**PURPOSE**

Guide employees who suspect an account compromise, phishing attempt, malware infection, unauthorized access, or another possible security incident.

**COMMON SIGNS OF A SECURITY INCIDENT**

Examples include:
- Unexpected login notifications or authentication requests.
- Suspicious emails, links, or attachments.
- Unknown activity on an employee's account.
- Files or applications behaving unusually after opening a suspicious file.
- Lost or stolen work devices.
- Accidental disclosure of confidential information.
- Unauthorized access to systems or sensitive data.

These signs do not always confirm an incident, but they should be reported for investigation.

**IMMEDIATE ACTIONS**

1. Ask what happened and whether the suspicious activity is still occurring.
2. Advise the employee not to click suspicious links, open suspicious attachments, or approve unexpected authentication requests.
3. Direct the employee to report the incident through the organization's approved IT security channel as soon as possible.
4. If an account may be compromised, advise the employee to follow the organization's approved account recovery and security instructions.
5. If malware is suspected, do not recommend deleting evidence, installing unapproved tools, or attempting advanced removal. Follow the organization's incident response procedure.
6. Do not delay reporting while performing ordinary troubleshooting.

**INFORMATION TO COLLECT**

Collect only relevant details:
- Type of suspected incident.
- Approximate time it occurred.
- Affected account, device, or service.
- Description of suspicious activity.
- Relevant error messages or alert details.
- Actions the employee has already taken.

Do not request or include passwords, one-time verification codes, authentication tokens, or private keys. Do not ask the employee to forward suspicious content through an unapproved channel.

**ESCALATION**

Escalate suspected account compromise, malware, unauthorized access, data exposure, or other security incidents to the organization's IT security team or approved reporting channel promptly.

If the official reporting contact or procedure is not documented, do not invent one. Tell the employee to use their known official reporting channel or internal directory.

**CONVERSATION GUIDELINES**

- Remain calm, clear, and professional.
- Ask one relevant question at a time.
- Do not blame or embarrass the employee.
- Do not claim that an account has been secured or a threat removed without confirmation.
- Do not promise that the incident has been resolved.
- Do not expose sensitive information in a summary.
- Do not claim that a security report or ticket has been submitted unless the system confirms successful submission.
- If the employee reports immediate or ongoing suspicious activity, prioritize reporting over routine troubleshooting.
