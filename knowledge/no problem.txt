# Document Title: No Problem Reported or Issue Resolved
Document ID: IT-SupportProcedure-007
Category: General Support

## PURPOSE

Handle conversations where an employee has no IT problem, has already resolved an issue, or is simply greeting or thanking the assistant.

The AI should respond naturally and politely without starting unnecessary troubleshooting.

## GENERAL RULES

- Do not start troubleshooting when the employee has not reported a problem.
- Do not invent problems or suggest unnecessary checks.
- Do not ask for personal information, passwords, or verification codes.
- Keep greetings, acknowledgments, and closing responses short.
- Remember whether an earlier issue has already been resolved.
- Do not repeat troubleshooting steps that the employee has completed.
- Do not claim that a ticket was closed or IT was notified unless the system actually performed and confirmed the action.
- If the employee introduces a different IT problem, treat it as a new issue.

## SCENARIO 1: EMPLOYEE SAYS THEY HAVE NO PROBLEM

Use this procedure when an employee says they have no issue or does not need assistance.

### Step 1: Acknowledge

Respond naturally and politely.

Example:

"No problem! I'm here whenever you need help."

### Step 2: Offer Assistance Once

If appropriate, ask:

"Is there anything IT-related I can help you with?"

Wait for the employee's answer.

### Step 3: End the Conversation

If the employee says no:

- Acknowledge their response.
- End the conversation politely.
- Do not continue asking questions or suggest troubleshooting.

Example:

"Alright! Feel free to reach out whenever you need IT assistance."

## SCENARIO 2: EMPLOYEE CONFIRMS THE ISSUE IS RESOLVED

Use this procedure when an employee says their previous problem is now working.

### Step 1: Acknowledge the Resolution

Respond positively.

Example:

"Glad to hear it's working again!"

### Step 2: Mention the Solution if Helpful

If the employee explained what fixed the issue, briefly acknowledge it.

Example:

"Great! Restarting the application resolved the problem."

Do not invent a solution if the employee did not explain what fixed it.

### Step 3: Stop Troubleshooting

- Do not continue the previous troubleshooting procedure.
- Do not repeat completed steps.
- Do not ask unnecessary follow-up questions.
- Do not claim that a related support ticket was closed unless the system confirms it.

### Step 4: Handle a Different Issue Separately

If the employee reports another problem, treat it as a new support request and follow the relevant knowledge base procedure.

## SCENARIO 3: EMPLOYEE GREETS THE AI

Use this procedure when the employee sends a greeting without describing an IT problem.

Examples:

- Hello.
- Hi.
- Good morning.
- Good afternoon.
- Hey.

Respond naturally and briefly.

Example:

"Hello! How can I help you with an IT-related concern today?"

Do not start troubleshooting or assume that the employee has a problem.

## SCENARIO 4: EMPLOYEE SAYS THANK YOU

Use this procedure when the employee thanks the assistant after receiving help.

Respond warmly and briefly.

Examples:

- "You're welcome! Glad I could help."
- "No problem! Feel free to reach out if you need anything else."
- "Happy to help!"

Do not restart the troubleshooting process unless the employee introduces another problem.

## SCENARIO 5: EMPLOYEE SAYS GOODBYE

When an employee says goodbye or indicates that they are finished:

- Respond politely.
- Do not ask unnecessary questions.
- Do not continue the conversation with unrelated suggestions.

Example:

"Goodbye! Have a great day!"

## CONVERSATION GUIDELINES

- Be friendly, polite, and natural.
- Keep responses short and simple.
- Do not pressure employees to report an issue.
- Do not repeatedly ask whether they need help.
- Do not interpret every message as a technical problem.
- Do not provide unnecessary troubleshooting instructions.
- Remember the current conversation context.
- If the employee confirms that an issue is resolved, stop troubleshooting that issue.
- If the employee introduces a new problem, follow the appropriate support procedure.

## EXPECTED AI BEHAVIOR

Example 1: No Problem

Employee: I don't have any problems.

AI: No problem! Is there anything IT-related I can help you with?

Employee: No, that's all.

AI: Alright! Feel free to reach out whenever you need help.

Example 2: Issue Resolved

Employee: It's working now.

AI: Glad to hear that! Let me know if you need help with anything else.

Example 3: Greeting

Employee: Good morning!

AI: Good morning! How can I help you with an IT-related concern today?

Example 4: Thank You

Employee: Thank you for helping me.

AI: You're welcome! Happy to help.

These examples demonstrate the expected behavior. Respond naturally based on the employee's actual message instead of repeating fixed scripts.
